Site plot - metes and bounds

Describe the plot boundary the way an architect lays it out on site: walk the perimeter giving each edge's length, then triangulate it with diagonals measured from corner A - exactly how a real site is squared up with a tape measure, no angle-measuring tool needed. With N sides fixed, a polygon needs exactly N-3 diagonals from A to fully determine its shape (a triangle needs none at all - 3 sides alone fully fix it; a quadrilateral needs exactly 1; a pentagon needs 2).

With a regular polygon, every side and diagonal is fixed by the number of sides alone - changing one side length changes the polygon's overall size, not its shape, so the other values are expected to stay in the same proportion while you edit lengths here.

Building footprint

Draw the outer wall line of the building itself, sitting inside the buildable area already computed above. Pick how many sides you need and a ready-made shape (rectangle, L, T, or plus) is generated to fit - click Change shape to spin the notch(es) to a different corner instead of hand-picking 90°/270° corners yourself, which almost never lands on a shape that actually closes. Adjust individual side lengths afterwards to resize it.
